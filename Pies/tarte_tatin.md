# Tarte Tatin

*Upside-down caramelized apple tart: apples cooked in butter caramel on the stove, baked under a pastry lid, then flipped.*

**Yield:** one 25 cm (10-inch) tart, 6 to 8 slices | **Active time:** 50 minutes | **Bake:** 20 to 25 minutes at 375°F (190°C)

Adapted from [Tarte Tatin, Meilleur du Chef](https://www.meilleurduchef.com/fr/recette/tarte-tatin.html), converted to grams and US ingredients.

---

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gala or Pink Lady apples, whole (8 to 10 medium) | 1600 g |
| Unsalted butter | 150 g |
| Granulated sugar | 150 g |
| [Pâte brisée sucrée](../Pastry/pate_brisee_sucree.md), about half a batch | 200 g |
| Ground cinnamon (optional) | 1 g |

### To finish (optional)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Apricot jam or apple jelly, warmed and strained | 100 g |

Serve with crème fraîche or vanilla ice cream.

---

## Method

1. **Make the dough.** Make half a batch of the [pâte brisée sucrée](../Pastry/pate_brisee_sucree.md) and chill it for 30 minutes.
2. **Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C).** Set a rack in the middle.
3. **Prep the apples.** Peel them, cut them in half, and scoop out the cores with the tip of a paring knife.
4. **Make the caramel.** Melt the butter in a 25 cm (10-inch) oven-safe skillet over medium heat. While it is still only partly melted, scatter the sugar evenly over it and stir gently until combined. Cook to a brown caramel, then take the pan off the heat. Stop before it turns dark, or it will taste bitter.
5. **Pack in the apples.** Stand the halves upright in the warm caramel, as tightly as they will go, since they shrink as they cook. Cut any remaining apples into smaller pieces and fill the gaps on top. Dust with cinnamon, if using.
6. **Start the apples on the stove (10 minutes).** Return the skillet to medium or medium-high heat so the apples begin to cook and soak up caramel.
7. **Cover with pastry.** On a floured counter, roll the dough to about 3 mm thick and cut a round about 2.5 cm (1 inch) wider than the skillet. Lay it over the hot apples and tuck the overhang down inside the pan. Press it gently onto the apples with your palms without tearing it, then cut a small steam vent in the center.
8. **Bake (20 to 25 minutes at 375°F / 190°C).** Bake until the pastry is cooked through and caramel is bubbling up the sides.
9. **Flip.** Set a rimmed serving plate upside down over the skillet. Holding the two together with dry towels, turn them over in one quick, firm motion. Set the plate down and lift off the skillet, watching for splashing caramel.
10. **Serve warm.** If serving it plain, brush lightly with the warmed jam so it does not dry out.

## Notes

- **Apples:** Gala and Pink Lady give off less water, which keeps the tart from going soggy. The original also suggests Reine des Reinettes, which is hard to find in the US.
- **Caramel:** the butter may separate from the caramel on the stove. It comes back together in the oven.
- **Pan:** the original uses a 24 cm stovetop-safe Tatin mold. A 25 cm (10-inch) cast iron or stainless skillet with an oven-safe handle is the closest US equivalent.
- **Oven:** the original gives a range of 350 to 400°F (180 to 200°C). Judge by the pastry rather than the clock; a deeper color can take up to 40 minutes.
- **Dough:** the original calls for 200 g of pâte brisée or pâte sucrée. One batch of the pâte brisée sucrée makes two lids; the other half freezes for a month.
- **Eggs:** the apples and caramel have none. The crust contains egg, 50 g to 200 g of flour, fully baked.
- **Glaze:** the original uses nappage blond, a French pastry glaze. Warmed, strained apricot jam is the usual US stand-in.
- **Variation:** a pinch of flaky sea salt in the caramel.
- **Conversions:** the apple weight and the cinnamon amount are estimates. The original gives a count of apples and no amount for cinnamon.
